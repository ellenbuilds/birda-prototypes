## points-and-levels

The clickable prototype of the **chosen** design — built from the Figma board *Iteration 1: Flows* ([`DXdW1BgO9HmKqJZN5mQbdV`](https://www.figma.com/design/DXdW1BgO9HmKqJZN5mQbdV/The-Game-of-Birda?node-id=489-4414), section `489:4414`). One self-contained `v1/index.html`, no build step, from `_resources/template.html`. The **viewport is 402×874 — the Figma frame size** — so every coordinate lifted from the board or the animation demos is used 1:1.

Three flows, eleven screens:

**Onboarding**
1. **Your collection starts with one bird** (`489:5608`) — a swipeable deck of 6 starter cards (drag left, or tap to flick to the next). The button follows the top card — *"Start with the dunnock"*. The last card is **"Not sure?" / *Nullae aves***, whose button switches to the **outline style** and reads *"See the first bird again"*; tapping it flies the whole pile back in from the left and restacks it (260 ms, staggered 45 ms per card so the pile builds up from the back). Whichever bird you pick becomes card #1 and carries through every later screen.
   **The deck doesn't loop.** Slots past the end of it are genuinely empty, so the pile thins from three cards to two to one as you reach the last one, and trying to swipe left there gives the card a small decaying nudge (−7 / +5 / −2 px over 380 ms) instead of wrapping round. Swiping right on the first card does the same.
   The carousel motion is transcribed keyframe-for-keyframe from the Figma timeline **"motion: starter card carousel"** (`514:3459`, read with `get_motion_context`): three cards sit at the same spot fanned only by rotation; the top card flings out to `(-331.5, 9.2)` at `-5.75°` over 236 ms, then travels back in over 244 ms to land at the bottom of the pile, while card 2 promotes to top (77 ms delay, 235 ms, ease-in-out) and card 3 to the middle (153 ms, 275 ms), and the selected dot slides one 16 px pitch (115 ms, 235 ms, linear). `translate` and `rotate` are animated as separate properties because the timeline gives them different curves. The two resting slot-transform sets alternate on each advance, which is what gives the pile its hand-shuffled look. Figma's 397 ms lead-in, hold to 2 s and boomerang loop are preview framing — here it's one-shot per swipe.
   **Swiping right plays the identical timeline backwards** — the previous card comes out from under the pile and returns to the top. The traveller's curves are asymmetric, so it's the same keyframes with `direction: 'reverse'`; the shuffle pair and the dot are symmetric (ease-in-out / linear) so they just take the mirrored delay, `480 − (delay + duration)`. A rightward drag never moves the top card — it stays on its resting mark and the reverse simply starts once the drag passes 60 px, so the only thing that moves is the card coming in from the left. Dragging left still carries the top card under the finger.
   Gesture plumbing: photos are `pointer-events:none` + `user-drag:none` so a drag never turns into a native image drag; leaving the phone frame mid-drag releases the card at the frame edge rather than stranding it (`pointercancel` / `lostpointercapture` are handled too); drag distance is divided by the shell's fit scale so the threshold is in design px regardless of window size; and the swipe handlers are bound once per card element, gated on "am I currently the top card", so cards cycling through the top slot don't accumulate duplicate listeners.
   Picking a bird **pushes** to screen 2 iOS-style: the new screen slides in from the right over 420ms on `cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1)` with a shadow under its leading edge, while screen 1 parallaxes out to −30%. `pushTo()` settles on whichever of the animation finishing, being cancelled, or a 480ms timer comes first, so the nav can't be stranded mid-transition; `show()` cancels any in-flight screen animation and clears leftover transforms.
2. **Look who just landed in your card collection** (`585:1671` / `609:10916`) — the bronze collection card with the acorn/leaf **Confetti pop**, and *"Log a … whenever you see one to upgrade your card. Only 4 more to go until silver!"*. Copy sits at 94 like screen 1, then a **48px** gap to the card and another to the note (copy 94–158, card 206–619, note 667–739, button 794–842).
   The card **arrives face-down and spins round to its front**, showing the reverse (`imgs/cards/card-back.png`, the shared bronze back) as it starts. **Never put a keyframe at −90°:** the card is edge-on and therefore invisible there — `backface-visibility` also swaps faces at that point — so it has to cross it at its fastest. A stop at −90° gives the incoming curve a near-zero exit slope, meaning the rotation slows to a crawl exactly where nothing is drawn, and the card appears to vanish and then pop back in. One accelerating sweep instead.
   The beats: **−180° → +22° over 420ms**, crossing edge-on at 248ms at 0.65°/ms — about **18ms, one frame, spent within ±6° of invisible**. Once the front is showing it **rocks to a stop over 620ms**: `−16° → +10° → −6° → +3° → 0`, each swing about two-thirds the last and quicker than the last (175/145/120/100/80ms), with ease-in-out at the extremes so it decelerates into each turn and accelerates back through centre. A single there-and-back reads as stiff; the decay is what makes it feel sprung. Total 1040ms, burst as the flip lands it face-on. A `scale 1.16 → 1` pop rides on the outer element so it can't clash with the rotation, settling as the card comes face-on; scale must not join the rocking, since dipping below 1 reads as the card stamping down. Times in `SPIN_KEYS` are absolute ms so the beats are readable. Afterwards it keeps a **slow left-right sway** — `rotateY` 0 → −6° → 0 → +6° → 0 over 6.4s, keyframed back to 0 at both ends so the loop joins seamlessly, and skipped under `prefers-reduced-motion`. `stopSway()` runs on every screen change, since an endless animation would otherwise keep going on a hidden screen.
   **Screen 10 is the same screen in a different context**, so it shares all of it — spacing (`1568:16227` puts its copy and card in exactly the same places), the reverse, the spin, the sway and the burst. The only difference is that its note is hidden on the frame, so it has none.
   The delay is handed to WAAPI with `fill: 'backwards'` rather than run off a timer, so the first keyframe holds the card out of sight until it spins in; a timer then cancels the entry so the card can't be left hidden or mid-spin if the animation never runs.
   The reverse sits on the back of the flip via `transform: rotateY(180deg)` + `backface-visibility: hidden`, which is what keeps the artwork from coming out mirrored.
3. **You've reached level 2** (`648:5701`) — the level track (Level 1 ✓ → the level-2 patch → 3 · 120XP), the bar filling to **🍃50XP**, *"First card bonus"*, and the *"You're ahead of the flock"* row. Motion ported beat-for-beat from `animation-demos/level-up-demo.html` (screen A).
4. **Great work! Now let's go birding.** (`489:6159`) — the first card goal: 1/4 collected, +40XP bonus, one filled slot and three `???` slots.

**Explore map** (screen 5, `489:6849` / `1524:5602`)
The hub: the real Minsmere map, search / layers / locate controls, the production **ChunkyButton** pair (Identify + Log), and a bottom sheet holding the **XP chip** (`🍃50XP · LVL 2`) and **cards chip** (`1/4 cards`), a targets carousel, and the 5-item tab bar. The heading reads **Next targets** before you've logged anything and **Birds likely nearby** afterwards. Tapping the cards chip opens the **card goal sheet** (screen 6, `1525:14298`).

**Logging your first bird** (screens 7–11)
`+` or **Identify/Log** → **log method** sheet (`489:4488`) → **What did you see?** (`489:4498`, layout reused from `PEX-43/v2`) → **Review Sighting** (`1568:15606`, also from `PEX-43/v2`) → **Save** → **New card found** + confetti (`1568:15949`) → **Don't stop there!** card-upgrade explainer (`1573:18999`) — the card fills its bar and 3D-flips **Bronze → Silver → Gold → Shiny** with a sheen sweep on each flip, ported from `animation-demos/upgrade-demo.html` (its structure, gradients, clip-path frame, schedule and `speed: 2` default, 1:1) → back to the map, where the **XP toast** springs in (*"Sighting saved: 🍃+18XP"*, bar 50→68/120XP, the chip popping and ticking up), and once it has gone the **card toast** follows (*"2/4 new cards found"*, dots filling, chip → 2/4) — the hand-off the board annotates as *"After XP toast dismissed"*. Toast motion is the shipped variant (**1d "Update on arrival"**) from `animation-demos/toast-demo.html`.

Only the **happy path** of the post-save chain is built. The other branches on the board — *Card upgraded?*, *Card goal completed?*, *User level up?* — and the session/multi-bird band are not wired up yet.

### Notes for the next pass
- **Text renders with grayscale antialiasing** (`-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased` / `-moz-osx-font-smoothing: grayscale` on `body`). macOS Chrome and Safari default to subpixel antialiasing, which thickens strokes and makes Medium read closer to SemiBold than it does in Figma. Nothing else in this repo sets it yet, so add it to `_resources/template.html` if the same mismatch shows up elsewhere.
- **Type and vertical rhythm** are read off the frames with `get_metadata`, not inferred from renders. Screen headlines are Rubik **Medium (500)** at 24/32, subheads 400 at 16/24 with `0.2px` tracking; the **status bar is the shared `_resources/ui-elements/iphone-status-bar.png`** asset (804×124, transparent, so it sits over the map as well) drawn at 402×62 on every screen, rather than time-plus-icons rendered in HTML; buttons are 48px tall, 322 wide, 32px clear of the bottom. Screen 1 matches frame **`489:5608`** exactly — copy block 32px below the status bar (title 94–158, subhead 162–210 with a 4px gap), the stack's top card 244–657 at **296×413**, the ten 8px dots on a 16px pitch at 735, button 794–842 — and the card's internals match its `starter card` instance too (name 24, latin 52, photo 224×169 at 85, description 270, divider and footer at 365).
  Note `489:5608` is the canonical 402×874 screen; the motion frame `514:3459` is 418 wide and sits ~18px higher, so measure spacing from `489:5608` and take only the keyframes from the motion frame.
- **The starter card** matches its component (`get_design_context` on `514:3085`): 8px radius, a 1px white edge drawn as an inset ring so it costs no layout, the `#FCFCFC → #FAFAFA` gradient and the same `0 28px 52px` shadow as the collection card. Name 20px Medium `#4C5980`, binomial 14px italic `#4C5980`, description and "Likely near you" 14px `#636E90`, rarity a **pill** (the map's target-card badges stay 4px rounded rects). Description height is left to the copy — three of the nine run to four lines and still clear the divider by 18px. The **"Not sure?"** card keeps the divider and footer with nothing in it (a `min-height` on the footer holds the divider on 365 for all ten cards) and uses the shared **`_resources/ui-elements/bird-placeholder.svg`** at 112px, which carries its own `#D6D9E0` fill.
  The one deliberate departure: Figma fills the 224×169 photo box using the original photo at `object-fit: cover` with `mix-blend-multiply`, which hides its white background. This uses the keyed cutouts at `contain` instead, so the bird sits a little smaller in the frame but is never cropped.
- **Assets** live in `v1/imgs/`. `map.jpg` is the board's own Mapbox image, cropped to the exact region the Figma frame shows. `level-patch-2.svg` and the five `conf-*.svg` confetti sprites were extracted from the animation-demo bundles, so they're the same artwork. `level-patch-3.svg` is in there unused, ready for the ongoing level-up branch. The XP feather is an inline SVG path in the file.
- **`imgs/birds/*.png`** are the shared `_resources/bird-photos/` cutouts with their **white studio background keyed out** — the shared files are lossy WebP with no alpha, so they'd show as white boxes on the peach card. A few have small white remnants where the original white was enclosed by the bird (under a leg, on a perch).
- **Watch for class-name collisions.** The card's progress bar is `.tbar .tprog`, deliberately not `.track`, because the level-up timeline owns `.track` and declares `width: 402px` — the card's bar silently inherited it and ran the full width of the phone, hidden only by the card's `overflow: hidden`. It also meant the fill never looked full, since a "246px = complete" bar only covered 61% of a 402px track.
- **The card's name container sizes to the name.** On the component the metal frame is the 8px ring *unioned* with a "name container" rounded rect — 102×33 at (8,8), sized for the 78px word "Dunnock", so anything longer ("House sparrow" is 133px) spills onto the frame. The clip-path is therefore generated by `fitCardNames()` with the container's right edge as a parameter: 8px before the name, 16px after, sized purely from the text. It measures with **`offsetWidth`, not `getBoundingClientRect()`** — the shell is scaled to fit the window and a client rect comes back multiplied by that scale, which silently sizes the container too small on any window where the shell isn't 1:1. It re-runs on `document.fonts.ready` (text measures short until Rubik lands) and covers all four faces of the upgrade card. Verified identical at shell scales 1.0, 0.49 and 0.33.
- **Card artwork** goes in `imgs/cards/` and is registered in the `CARD_ART` map in the file. Only `robin.png` (the sticker from the upgrade demo) is real artwork so far; every other species falls back to its keyed-out photo inside the same 202×202 box, with the sticker outline drawn in CSS. Drop a new file in, add one line to `CARD_ART`, and that species gets the proper card. `holo.jpg` is the Shiny tier's holographic texture.
- **`?seek=<ms>`** freezes the animated screens (2, 3, 10) at one moment instead of playing them — useful for grabbing a still of a specific beat.
- `index.html#N` opens straight on screen N; `←`/`→` step through.
- **"What did you see?" and "Birds likely nearby" are fixed lists** — birds likely near your location, not filtered by what you've already collected, so they read the same every run. That makes three save outcomes reachable, and all three are wired up: a **new species** takes the full card path (New card found → Don't stop there → XP toast then card toast); **a bird already in your collection** and **an unidentified sighting** both take the board's other route — XP but no card, so they skip the celebrations and land back on the map with the XP toast alone. That matches the board's *"Species logged before?"* gate and its *"N/A: unidentified sighting → straight to the 'user level up?' check"* note.

Patterns mirror the production app (`/Users/ellen/code/app`, React Native + Expo), reimplemented as plain HTML/CSS/JS for a browser-openable prototype.

Flow (from the Figma "Route 2 · Collecting sets" page). The Figma **P2 "creating your account" loader is intentionally omitted** — it added little:

7. **Home** — the app home screen (map + "65 known species in this area" + Next Targets cards). The tab opens with a banner showing the golden onboarding egg (now **cracked**) and a live **countdown timer** ticking down from 21 hours ("Your golden egg is hatching"). Tabs are sticky; the other tabs' contents aren't prototyped. A bottom tab bar (Explore · Community · **+** · Challenges · Profile) sits at the foot of the screen — its **+** opens the log-a-bird flow (screen 8), as does the Home screen's Log button and its nav **+**. Two more for Gold." Card name + photo match the logged bird. **View your … sticker** → Home; **Back to collection** → the album.

## route-3-mastery
A working prototype of the **Route 3 · Mastery & identity** flow — *"the recognition loop"* (built from the Figma "Route 3 · Mastery and identity" page — 10 screens, three bands: onboarding → know what you know · log → grow your confidence · track your mastery). The Figma frames are a low-fidelity *Static flow · DRAFT* (emoji placeholders, flat lists); this prototype keeps that flow and copy but **polishes it to Route 2's fidelity** — Rubik, brand tokens, real bird photos from the shared folder, smooth transitions. The species-select and Review Sighting screens are reused from Route 2 / `PEX-43/v2`; the home reuses Route 2's map-home idea (a CSS local-map hero) with a Route 3 **"Your birds" mastery section** layered in. The core idea: **logging is the game** — every log earns XP and levels up your *mastery* of that species (Spotter → Learner → Identifier → Knower → Expert). You're not collecting things; you're becoming a better birder.

A shared **mastery-rank pill** (tier colour scale) and progress bars run through every screen, with a deliberate two-tier bar language: **user/overall XP bars are gold** (thicker, warm amber gradient — the Home level card and the rank-reveal meter) while **per-bird mastery bars are thin and cool** (tier-coloured: blue Learner, grey Spotter, etc.), so "your XP" reads as visually distinct from "this bird's mastery". The single `index.html` carries the picked species name/rank/photo and the mastery copy through Review → Payoff → Species page via `selectSpecies()`.

A **walkthrough context panel** sits beside the phone (hidden below 900px wide). It leads with the route thesis (*"Birda is where you learn to truly know the birds around you… every ID levels up your mastery, Spotter → Expert"*) and then, per screen, names the **game mechanics in play** — mapped from the Discovery FigJam board (the Route 3 row of *"Aligning on routes to explore"*, cross-checked against the full *"Mapping game mechanics"* inventory): **CORE — Levels & XP** (the loop) plus the supporting layers **Endowed Progress, Explicit Progress Meters, Near-Miss Nudges, Streaks (+ Freeze), Variable/Surprise Rewards, Quests/Challenges, Badges, Identity/Profile Customisation, Ambient Social Proof, Geo & Time-Box Splits** — every label taken verbatim from the grid (no invented mechanics), marking CORE vs supporting, with a one-line *why it works* tying each stage to buy-in / habit / identity / retention. The generic, already-exists-in-app screens (Welcome, birding journey, the "What did you see?" species picker) show just the thesis + title; the **Home / map** screen is flagged as the **🎯 Testable slice** — the core log → reward loop to put in front of users first.
