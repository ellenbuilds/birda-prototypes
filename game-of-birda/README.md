## points-and-levels

The clickable prototype of the **chosen** design — built from the Figma board *Iteration 1: Flows* ([`DXdW1BgO9HmKqJZN5mQbdV`](https://www.figma.com/design/DXdW1BgO9HmKqJZN5mQbdV/The-Game-of-Birda?node-id=489-4414), section `489:4414`). One self-contained `v1/index.html`, no build step, from `_resources/template.html`. The **viewport is 402×874 — the Figma frame size** — so every coordinate lifted from the board or the animation demos is used 1:1.

**A sticker's white edge is a cut, not a glow — and it is baked in.** The cutouts read as blurry because of the outline, not the key: the bird's own pixels were always sharp (the alpha edge is 1px hard), but three chained `drop-shadow(0 0 3px #fff)` gave a diffuse halo fading into the card. Zero-blur chained shadows fix the blur, but they can't reach a border of any real thickness without going lumpy, since chained axis shadows dilate further on the diagonals. So the border is **baked into `imgs/birds/*.png`** by `scripts/stickerborder.py`, which also costs nothing while the card flips and sways. The CSS keeps only the cast shadow, now small and sharp (`0 2px 2px` at 28%).

That script does three things per cutout: **trim** to the opaque content, grow the canvas, then dilate the alpha as an octagon (alternating 4- and 8-neighbour passes approximate a disc) and fill white behind it. The radius is derived per bird — `R = target × long / (box − 2·target)` — so all ten land within 0.2px of the target in the card's 202px art box, and scale down proportionally everywhere else. The target is the script's one argument: the supplied robin card art measured **12 CSS px** against the old CSS outline's 4, and **8** is what we settled on. **The trim threshold matters:** at `alpha > 8` the robin measured its full 460×345 canvas, because some cutouts carry a faint wash of near-transparent pixels right across the frame; at 128 it is 323×329, which is the bird. Trimming is what makes every bird sit at the same size in the box — without it each one kept its own photo margins.

`CARD_ART` is now empty. Its only entry was the robin, and that one card having a baked border and shadow while the other nine had thin CSS ones is exactly what made it the odd one out; every bird goes down the same keyed path. The asset stays in `imgs/cards/` for when there is real per-bird card art. On near-white surfaces — the deck card, the map carousel, the review pin — the baked white is invisible: 255 against the deck's 252 paper.

**A toast must not survive leaving the map.** `show()` and `pushTo()` clear the pending `T()` timers, which includes the one that would have dismissed a toast — so the `.in` class stayed on and the toast was still hanging there on return. `tClear()` now also calls `resetToasts()`: classes back to base, the chip's pop and ripple cleared, and the counter's tween cancelled (it holds its rAF id in `TOAST.tween`, or it would keep writing over the value `paintChips()` restores). Cheap to run on every screen change, since the elements only exist on the map. The XP itself is already banked, so you come back to the new total on the chip with no toast.

Three flows, eleven screens:

**Onboarding**
1. **Your collection starts with one bird** (`489:5608`) — a swipeable deck of 6 starter cards (drag left, or tap to flick to the next). The button follows the top card — *"Start with the dunnock"*. The last card is **"Not sure?" / *Nullae aves***, whose button switches to the **outline style** and reads *"See the first bird again"*; tapping it flies the whole pile back in from the left and restacks it (260 ms, staggered 45 ms per card so the pile builds up from the back). Whichever bird you pick becomes card #1 and carries through every later screen.
   **The deck doesn't loop.** Slots past the end of it are genuinely empty, so the pile thins from three cards to two to one as you reach the last one, and trying to swipe left there gives the card a small decaying nudge (−7 / +5 / −2 px over 380 ms) instead of wrapping round. Swiping right on the first card does the same.
   The carousel motion is transcribed keyframe-for-keyframe from the Figma timeline **"motion: starter card carousel"** (`514:3459`, read with `get_motion_context`): three cards sit at the same spot fanned only by rotation; the top card flings out to `(-331.5, 9.2)` at `-5.75°` over 236 ms, then travels back in over 244 ms to land at the bottom of the pile, while card 2 promotes to top (77 ms delay, 235 ms, ease-in-out) and card 3 to the middle (153 ms, 275 ms), and the selected dot slides one 16 px pitch (115 ms, 235 ms, linear). `translate` and `rotate` are animated as separate properties because the timeline gives them different curves. The two resting slot-transform sets alternate on each advance, which is what gives the pile its hand-shuffled look. Figma's 397 ms lead-in, hold to 2 s and boomerang loop are preview framing — here it's one-shot per swipe.
   **Swiping right plays the identical timeline backwards** — the previous card comes out from under the pile and returns to the top. The traveller's curves are asymmetric, so it's the same keyframes with `direction: 'reverse'`; the shuffle pair and the dot are symmetric (ease-in-out / linear) so they just take the mirrored delay, `480 − (delay + duration)`. A rightward drag never moves the top card — it stays on its resting mark and the reverse simply starts once the drag passes 60 px, so the only thing that moves is the card coming in from the left. Dragging left still carries the top card under the finger.
   Gesture plumbing: photos are `pointer-events:none` + `user-drag:none` so a drag never turns into a native image drag; leaving the phone frame mid-drag releases the card at the frame edge rather than stranding it (`pointercancel` / `lostpointercapture` are handled too); drag distance is divided by the shell's fit scale so the threshold is in design px regardless of window size; and the swipe handlers are bound once per card element, gated on "am I currently the top card", so cards cycling through the top slot don't accumulate duplicate listeners.
   Picking a bird **pushes** to screen 2 iOS-style, and *Add card to my collection* → 3 and *Continue* → 4 push on the same way: the new screen slides in from the right over 420ms on `cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1)` with a shadow under its leading edge, while screen 1 parallaxes out to −30%. `pushTo()` settles on whichever of the animation finishing, being cancelled, or a 480ms timer comes first, so the nav can't be stranded mid-transition; `show()` cancels any in-flight screen animation and clears leftover transforms.
2. **Look who just landed in your card collection** (`585:1671` / `609:10916`) — the bronze collection card with the acorn/leaf **Confetti pop**, and *"Log a … whenever you see one to upgrade your card. Only 4 more to go until silver!"*. Copy sits at 94 like screen 1, then a **48px** gap to the card and another to the note (copy 94–158, card 206–619, note 667–739, button 794–842).
   The card **arrives face-down and spins round to its front**, showing the reverse (`imgs/cards/card-back.png`, the shared bronze back) as it starts. **Never put a keyframe at −90°:** the card is edge-on and therefore invisible there — `backface-visibility` also swaps faces at that point — so it has to cross it at its fastest. A stop at −90° gives the incoming curve a near-zero exit slope, meaning the rotation slows to a crawl exactly where nothing is drawn, and the card appears to vanish and then pop back in. One accelerating sweep instead.
   The beats: **−180° → +32°**, crossing edge-on at 230ms at speed — about **20ms, one frame, spent within ±6° of invisible**. The flip carries its speed, so it has to **start easing off well before the extreme** or it slams into the tilt and stops dead; an intermediate stop at `+18°/380ms` shapes that, and the last 14° then glide in — 0.75°/ms at 300ms, 0.28 at 400ms, effectively stopped by 490ms. It is not another tilt, just the same move shaped. Once the front is showing there are **two tilts, wide and languid**: **+32° right**, then **−24° left over 480ms**, then a **600ms settle to centre** — so the card lolls to a stop rather than snapping. Two big slow tilts, not a flurry of small ones: more swings read as a mechanical rattle, and a short shallow there-and-back reads as stiff. Total 1580ms, burst as the flip lands it face-on, and the footer button **rises in** once the tilts are done — 14px lift on ease-out-cubic over 400ms with the fade completing halfway, the same entry the level-up screen gives its Continue button (`riseIn()`). A `scale 1.16 → 1` pop rides on the outer element so it can't clash with the rotation, settling as the card comes face-on; scale must not join the tilting, since dipping below 1 reads as the card stamping down. Times in `SPIN_KEYS` are absolute ms so the beats are readable. The upgrade explainer hands over to the **same sway** once its tiers are done. Two things it needs that the new-card screens don't: the card finishes at **540°** (three flips), so `startSway()` takes a `base` and turns about that rather than snapping back to 0 and showing the bronze front again; and the last flip is a **1s CSS transition** kicked off at `UP_FLIPS[2]`, which — because the timeline runs at `UP_SPEED` — outlasts the timeline itself in wall clock (settles at 6550ms against the timeline's 6300). `SWAY_IN` derives that gap rather than hard-coding it, so the sway begins at 6670ms and never clips the final flip.

Afterwards it keeps a **slow left-right sway** — `rotateY` 0 → −6° → 0 → +6° → 0 over 6.4s, keyframed back to 0 at both ends so the loop joins seamlessly, and skipped under `prefers-reduced-motion`. `stopSway()` runs on every screen change, since an endless animation would otherwise keep going on a hidden screen.
   **Screen 10 is the same screen in a different context**, so it shares all of it — spacing (`1568:16227` puts its copy and card in exactly the same places), the reverse, the spin, the sway and the burst. The only difference is that its note is hidden on the frame, so it has none.
   The delay is handed to WAAPI with `fill: 'backwards'` rather than run off a timer, so the first keyframe holds the card out of sight until it spins in; a timer then cancels the entry so the card can't be left hidden or mid-spin if the animation never runs.
   The reverse sits on the back of the flip via `transform: rotateY(180deg)` + `backface-visibility: hidden`, which is what keeps the artwork from coming out mirrored.

It lives on **its own layer** (`.tabshape`) rather than on `.tabbar`, because the clip would otherwise eat the FAB and the labels — and the labels need `position:relative; z-index:1`, since a positioned sibling paints over static ones whatever the DOM order. **The shadow has to be filtered on a wrapper, not on the clipped element**: filters are applied *before* `clip-path`, so filtering the clipped box clips the shadow away with it — which looks exactly like the shadow silently not existing. The clip sits on `::before`, the `filter` on the parent. Its value is the export's own `feDropShadow` — dy −5, stdDeviation 15, `#2D3142` at 10% — i.e. `drop-shadow(0 -5px 30px …)`, and it now tracks the frame's gradient to within 1.5%.

The tab **glyphs were sitting 17–33% short** of their 20px boxes: each `viewBox` is now squared onto that icon's own ink bounds (measured with `getBBox()`), so the widest dimension fills 20 — Explore 20×19.9, Community 20×15, Challenges 18.8×20, Profile 20×20, against the frame's 20 / 20×16 / 20×17.8 / 20. The FAB's plus was 16 in a 24px box and is now the frame's 19.5. The Profile dot had a hard-coded `#C4C7D7`; it takes `currentColor` like the rest. The Community glyph is still two figures where the frame has three — a drawing difference, not a size one.

**Every blur number Figma hands you is a standard deviation; both CSS `box-shadow` and CSS `filter: drop-shadow()` take a radius ≈ 2σ — so double it.** Reading the exported `drop-shadow(0 5px 7.5px …)` straight into `box-shadow` made every soft shadow on this screen about half as wide as the frame's — most visibly the FAB, which looked like a hard ring instead of a glow. Corrected across the screen: FAB `0 5px 15px rgba(31,135,254,.5)`, map controls `0 2px 4px rgba(0,0,0,.25)`, toast `0 4px 8px rgba(0,0,0,.1)`, and the tab bar `0 -2px 24px rgba(20,22,30,.10)` (the export's 35px of top bleed implies σ≈12). The chunky buttons' `0 5px 0` edges are unaffected — σ=0 either way.

The targets row is a **horizontal carousel**: it breaks out of the sheet's 24px padding (`margin:0 -24px; padding:0 24px`) so it scrolls the full frame width with that padding as the start/end inset — 499px of content in 402, i.e. 97px of travel, landing the third card's right edge exactly on the 24px margin. Scrollbar hidden, `overscroll-behavior-x:contain` so a flick doesn't rubber-band the page.

The floating **Log** button uses the shared `#birdFilled` placeholder — the frame's `icon/crow` is the same glyph, and its 10% vertical inset in a 16px box is exactly the asset's own proportions, so it drops in at `viewBox="0 0 78 78"` recoloured to `#1E87FD`. (The paths in `<defs>` carry no `fill` attribute, which is what lets `fill` on the `<svg>` reach them — a `fill` on the `<path>` would beat inheritance and silently ignore every recolour.)

Two class collisions bit here, both the same shape as the earlier `.track` one: the chip's **LVL 2** pill was named `.lvl`, so it inherited `width:402px; margin-left:-40px` from the screen-3 level track — `.chip .lvl` won on the properties it declared but not on the ones it didn't — and blew the cards chip clean off the screen. It is `.lvlpill` now. **When a small element shares a name with a full-width layout container, check the properties the specific rule doesn't set.**

**Log method is its own screen, not an overlay** (`489:4488`)
The board's node for this one is only a **50%-opacity screenshot of the app** over a dark rect, so there is no vector design to read — the geometry comes from the full-opacity screenshot instead (402pt at 2×, so it maps 1:1 onto the frame). Plain white, no map behind it and no scrim: three cards at x=24, **354×121**, **8px radius**, from y=183 on a **24px** gutter — `#DEFAEC`/`#009551`, `#E8E7FF`/`#7165E2`, `#FBEBE2`/`#FE9857` — each with a **64px** tile at x=44, **vertically centred**, and the text block **top-aligned at 17**. **Fit type by measuring rendered string widths, not cap heights.** Thresholded ink gave cap heights implying 19px/600 titles and 16px/400 descriptions. Measuring the eleven actual line widths off the screenshot and fitting candidate sizes *inside the prototype page* gave **18px/700** and **14px/400** — rms 1.0 and 1.3px, where 19/600 and 16/400 were out by 4 and 21. A strict ink threshold shrinks thin strokes far more than thick ones, so it biases small and biases the regular text worse than the bold. Two traps on the way: a scratch measuring page whose font stack was just `Rubik` (no fallback) silently measured a **different font** and produced numbers that disagreed with the prototype — always measure in the page you are matching, and assert `document.fonts.check()`; and **Chrome's UA stylesheet resets `letter-spacing` on form controls**, so the body's global `.2px` never reached this screen's text, since each card is a `<button>`.

The text column is **196px**, not the 221 the padding implies — swept 192→204 and only 196–197 reproduces all three cards' line breaks. **Only *Log a Sighting* is wired up** — it goes to the species picker, and that is the whole logging flow this prototype covers. *Identify a Bird* and *Add a Session* are real features we aren't prototyping, so they are `div`s rather than `button`s (no handler, `cursor:default`) and look identical to the live one.

The walker is the real asset — `_resources/ui-elements/person-walking-light.svg`, Font Awesome **Light**, so its strokes are filled outlines and it wants `fill:#fff; stroke:none` rather than the stroked treatment the other two use. It fills its 320×512 viewBox exactly, so 34px renders 21.2×34 against the reference's 21×34 — no tuning. The other two are still hand-drawn and sized individually (34 / 35.5) because each fills its box by a different amount; their inks land within 2px, though the bird faces the other way.

**Two bird glyphs, both real assets.** The hand-drawn `#crowPath` approximation is **deleted** — it has no users left. The **filled** placeholder (`#birdFilled`) is the frame's `icon/crow`, and carries the map's floating **Log** button (16px), the empty cards on the card-goal screen and sheet (78px), and the **new-card toast**'s mystery card (44px — the Figma instance is 44.266). The **outline** placeholder (`#birdSil`) is the one that belongs on **Review Sighting**: at 20px in ink-2 it sits at the same weight as the stroked calendar / pin / person icons beside it, where the filled one read as a blob. It is also the map-pin fallback for an unidentified sighting (30px in `#8890AE`), which is what that placeholder is for.

**Recolouring these needs a `style`, not a `fill=""` attribute.** `.row .ic svg` sets `fill:none` with a 1.5px stroke for the stroked icons, and **a CSS declaration beats a presentation attribute** — so `fill="var(--ink-2)" stroke="none"` on the element lost, and the bird was being *stroked* instead of filled. Since `#birdSil` is a filled path that draws its own outline, stroking it produced a doubled hairline scribble — which reads as "the icon looks wrong" rather than as a CSS precedence problem.

**No photo in the frame is draggable.** `.viewport img { user-drag:none }` — a stray drag lifts the image out of the page, which reads as the prototype breaking. The deck cards and the map carousel's photos also carry `pointer-events:none` so the card underneath gets the gesture instead.

**One ledger for the collection.** The chip and the card-goal sheet used to keep separate books: the chip incremented a `S.cards` counter on every arrival with a new card, while the sheet derived its contents as `[S.starter] + [S.logged]` — and `S.logged` only ever holds the *latest* bird. So the sheet could never show more than two cards, and by the third log the chip said 3 while the sheet showed 2 (dunnock + whatever was newest, the earlier one silently replaced). A **count counts sightings; the sheet counts species**, and the two drifted the moment those differed.

Everything now derives from `S.collected`, the species you actually hold, in the order found. That also fixed a second symptom of the same split: `isNewCard` only tested `S.logged !== S.starter`, so logging the *same* non-starter bird twice minted a duplicate card — the counter went up while the sheet stayed put. It now tests membership of `S.collected`, so a species you already hold earns XP but no second card. Walked through six logs including a repeat: chip and sheet agree at every step. Past the goal the chip keeps counting (5/4) while the sheet holds at four slots, which is as far as this prototype goes.

**Back out of the species list the way you came in.** Reached through the **+** and then *Log a Sighting*, its back button returns to the **log-method screen**; reached straight from the map's floating **Log** button — or from an empty card in the card-goal sheet — it returns to the **map**. `openPicker(from)` records the route and `pickerGoBack()` retraces it, and `goToMap()` clears it so nothing goes stale. The route survives going deeper: **+** → Log → pick → *Review Sighting* → back → back lands on the log-method screen, not the map. A deep link to the list has no route to retrace, so it falls back to the map.

**Nothing inert reads as clickable.** `button { cursor:pointer }` in the reset means every placeholder control invites a click it won't answer. **9 → 10 is a content push, not a screen push.** *Don't stop there!* is meant to read as the same screen taking new content, so **only the copy block and the footer travel** — a full frame width each on the push curve — while the card sits perfectly still. Two things make that work. The explainer's copy block is **88 tall, not 128**: the copy sits at the top of the block either way, and 88 is what puts its card on **206**, exactly where screen 9 leaves it (both 296×413 at x=53, and the bronze face `upgradeCardHTML` opens on is identical to the one `cardHTML` draws). And `armUpgrade()` now runs **synchronously** on entry rather than on the 80ms timer, so the card is already there for the first frame; the upgrade timeline starts `PUSH_MS + 80` in, once the content has landed.

Two traps in `pushContent()`:
- **The incoming screen's background has to come off for the duration.** `.screen` is opaque `var(--base)`, so activating screen 10 would hide the outgoing copy entirely and there would be nothing to see leaving. It is restored in `done()`, and `clearScreenAnims()` clears it too so an interrupted transition can't strand it.
- **The outgoing animations need `fill:'forwards'`.** With the default fill they snap back to 0 the instant they end, and here — unlike a real screen push — there is no opaque screen on top to hide that flash. `done()` cancels them and clears the inline translate.

- **Two identical cards stacked means two drop shadows.** The cards themselves cancel out perfectly, but `0 28px 52px rgba(20,22,24,.25)` compounding reads as one visibly darker shadow — measured **164 vs 202** three pixels under the card edge, about 15% darker. The outgoing card is hidden the moment the incoming one is painted (same task, so there is no frame with neither), which makes the overlap **pixel-identical** to a single card. `clearScreenAnims()` clears that `visibility` on every navigation, so no path can strand it hidden.

Sampling the transition at 0 / 210 / 420ms: the copy goes 40 → −344 → −362 while the incoming goes 442 → 58 → 40, and the card reads **53,206 at every one**.

A note on verifying this kind of thing: a screenshot taken "mid-transition" by pausing the animations is **not** mid-transition — `done()` still fires on its own timer and drops the outgoing screen, so the capture shows the settled state with the content frozen part-way. The shadow bug is invisible in such a shot. To see an overlap state, build it directly: activate both screens, run the incoming `onEnter`, and sample.

Only the **happy path** of the post-save chain is built. The other branches on the board — *Card upgraded?*, *Card goal completed?*, *User level up?* — and the session/multi-bird band are not wired up yet.
